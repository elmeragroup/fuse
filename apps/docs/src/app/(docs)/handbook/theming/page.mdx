<h2 id="three-axes">Three axes</h2>

A theme is exactly three coordinates, and nothing else:

- **Identity** — `neutral` or `branded`. Neutral is the grayscale system look, with the brand only in
  accents and logos; branded is the brand's full look and feel: its colours, surfaces, type and
  corners. Identity says nothing about density, so a branded customer dashboard can be dense and a
  neutral page comfortable.
- **Brand** — one of seven four-character codes. See
  [Brands & segments](/handbook/brands-and-segments).
- **Segment** — `private` or `company`.

Two of the seven brands are pinned to one segment, which is why there are twenty-four legal themes and
not twenty-eight. The `ThemeInput` type is a discriminated union that makes the four illegal
combinations unrepresentable in typed code — they are a compile error, not a runtime check.

<h2 id="how-it-resolves">How it resolves</h2>

Themes are pure CSS. Three data attributes on one element — `data-theme-identity`, `data-theme-brand`,
`data-theme-segment` — and the stylesheet's layers resolve the token values beneath them: shared
defaults, then brand pointers, then the neutral reset or the branded brand palette, then the one segment
delta that genuinely differs, and last the identity's corner set, which no colour scheme restates.

Dark rules restate only the roles a dark palette changes. The corners, the outline button's border, the
card edge and shadow, the selection row edges, the pressed toggle, the paragraph leading and the heading
scale keep their light values, so an override of one on the element that carries the theme attributes
holds in both colour schemes.

Because it is attributes plus cascade, brand is correct at first paint with JavaScript disabled, and
re-theming a subtree costs one wrapper element rather than a second stylesheet.

<h3 id="brand-neutral-ramp">Brand neutral ramp</h3>

Each brand rule also declares the brand neutral ramp, `--brand-neutral-50` to `--brand-neutral-950`, 50
the lightest: a grey at the hue of the brand's `--feature` colour, with chroma 0.01. Fjordkraft's
company segment takes the teal of its own feature colour. The ramp holds the same values in light and
dark and in both identities, because it holds raw tones, not roles. Every ramp shares one lightness per
step, so a step lines up roughly across brands. Equal lightness is not equal contrast, because luminance
also depends on hue and chroma, so measure the real text and surface pair you use, as Fuse's contrast
gates do. `:root` declares an untinted grey at the same lightness, and utilities such as
`bg-brand-neutral-100` read it.

Branded light themes point their neutral roles at the ramp, so fills and lines share the page's tint:
`--sidebar` and `--right-panel` at step 50, `--muted` and `--accent` at 100, `--input`,
`--sidebar-accent` and `--sidebar-border` at 200, and the `--border` hairline at 300. A role declares
the alias, such as `var(--brand-neutral-100)`, so overriding a step on the element that carries the
theme attributes moves every role that points at it. Neutral themes keep their untinted system greys,
and branded dark themes keep the neutrals they mix from each brand's dark card.

<h3 id="brand-palettes">Brand palettes</h3>

Each brand rule also declares two brand palettes on the same recipe, `--brand-1-50` to `--brand-1-950`
and `--brand-2-50` to `--brand-2-950`. A palette is built from a seed colour, a curated brand tone, and
passes through it: the step whose lightness is nearest the seed's holds the seed itself, unchanged.
Every other step sits on the ramp's shared lightness at the seed's hue and chroma, with the chroma
lowered only where sRGB cannot hold it at that lightness. Palette 1 is the brand colour, the brand's
`--feature` tone, so Fjordkraft's company segment is teal. Palette 2 is a second brand colour where the
brand curates one: Telinet's green and Gudbrandsdal Energi's orange. Every other brand has one colour,
so its `--brand-2-*` steps point at `--brand-1-*` step for step, and a role that points at palette 2
shows the brand colour. Like the neutral ramp, the palettes hold one value per brand and segment in
both schemes and identities, `:root` declares untinted greys, and utilities such as `bg-brand-1-800`
read them.

A role points at a palette and a step, not at a colour. Telinet's `--feature-foreground` is
`var(--brand-2-400)`, the step that holds its green. The brand's other tones, such as `--primary` and
the soft forms, stay curated colours, because they are hand-tuned. A product that needs a tone the
roles do not name takes a step instead of computing one. A selected sidebar row in the brand colour,
for example, takes a mid step and a light step rather than mixing `--primary` toward black:

{/* prettier-ignore */}
```css
/* After the Fuse stylesheet, on the element that carries the theme attributes.
   A step of the brand colour, not a mix computed from --primary. The palettes
   hold the same steps in light and dark, so one rule serves both schemes. Its
   four qualifiers match the weight of the most specific branded dark rule, and
   coming later in the cascade, it wins in every branded theme and scheme. */
.app-shell[data-theme-identity="branded"][data-theme-brand][data-theme-segment] {
  --sidebar-accent: var(--brand-1-600);
  --sidebar-accent-foreground: var(--brand-1-50);
}
```

In every branded theme and both schemes, step 50 on step 600 measures at least 5.61:1, and the
selected fill stands out from the sidebar by at least 5.59:1 in light and 2.11:1 in dark. A darker fill
such as step 800 would vanish into the dark sidebar, at 1.12:1. Equal steps line up in lightness across
brands but not in contrast, so measure the pair you choose in every brand you ship, as Fuse's contrast
gates measure its own role pairs. A page of the palettes with their measured step pairs is planned in
issue #434.

Corner rounding is part of the theme: each identity picks a corner set. A branded theme rounds cards
from the brand's `--radius` and spaces the `rounded-*` scale around it in 2px `--radius-step`
increments. Every standalone button rounds with `--radius-button`, which is a pill for Fjordkraft,
Fjordkraft Företag and Telinet. Field boxes, from Input and Select to InputGroup and the date fields,
round with `--radius-field`, which is `var(--radius)` in every theme, so they match the cards and
selection rows around them, and the parts inside a field round inside its corner. A button inside a
button group keeps a compact corner instead, and a button group rounds both its ends that way, even
when a field sits at one end. The standalone calendar keeps the reference's 4px corner, and the
checkbox caps its corner at 4px in every theme. The neutral identity rounds every outer element but
the checkbox with the one `--radius`. Its step is `0px` and its `--radius-button` and `--radius-field`
are `var(--radius)`, so a host rule that reads either role gets the same corner. To change the radius,
override `--radius` on the element that carries the theme attributes, which is `<html>` or a
`ThemeScope`. The theme rules resolve `--radius-button` and `--radius-field` there, so buttons and
fields move with the cards. On a plain wrapper the override reaches cards and neutral fields, but
buttons and branded fields keep the corners the theme element resolved, so set `--radius-field` beside
it. To give branded fields a corner of their own, override `--radius-field`. Neutral fields always
round with `--radius`, so the override does not reach them. Nested neutral surfaces share the radius,
and dialogs round like cards. Inner parts, such as menu rows, tabs and the buttons inside a field, are
the exception: see [Inner corners](#inner-corners).

A branded button that rounds as a pill would set a dense label against its curve, so at dense density a
branded button's label edges keep at least 12px of inline padding, at the xs, sm, default and lg sizes.
The edge beside a leading or trailing icon keeps its own inset, square icon buttons and buttons in a
button group are unchanged, and neutral themes and comfortable density, whose label padding is already
12px or more, keep their padding. A neutral `ThemeScope` inside a branded page declares its own floor
of 0px, so it does not inherit the branded one.

The outline button's border is part of the theme too. It reads `--button-outline` at
`--button-outline-width`. Branded themes draw a 2px ring in the text colour, so `--button-outline` is
`var(--foreground)`, and the button casts no shadow. The neutral identity keeps a 1px `var(--border)`
hairline with a small shadow, which only a 1px border casts.

A `Card` and a `card` Accordion item draw their 1px edge in `--card-border`. Neutral themes draw it in
`var(--border)`. Branded themes draw no edge: `--card-border` is a zero-alpha colour. A `Card` casts a
small shadow in `--card-shadow`, black at 5% in neutral themes and zero alpha in branded ones, so a
branded white card stands on the page tint by its fill alone. The border keeps its width and the
shadow its geometry, so the cards' inner corners and layout stay as they are. Without an edge or
shadow, a card on a `card`-coloured surface, such as a nested `Card`, has no outline. A `CheckboxCard`
is a control and the TextField `card` box is a field, so both keep their `var(--border)` edge and small
shadow. To change the card edge or shadow colour, override `--card-border` or `--card-shadow` on the
element that carries the theme attributes, which is `<html>` or a `ThemeScope`, where the theme rules
resolve them, or on any element around the cards. On a plain wrapper a `--border` override does not
reach cards.

The 1px line between a card's stacked `Card.Section` parts is `--card-section-divider`. Neutral themes
draw it in `var(--border)`, and branded themes in the page tint, `var(--background)`, so a card reads
as split into segments. Override it where you override `--card-border`.

A `RadioItem` or `CheckboxItem` row draws its 1px edge in `--selection-border`, and a checked row draws
it in `--selection-checked-border`. The line between the stacked rows of a vertical group is
`--selection-divider`, and above and below a checked row `--selection-checked-divider`. Neutral themes
draw the edge and the divider in `var(--border)` and box the checked row in `var(--primary)`. Branded
themes draw no outer edge: `--selection-border` is a zero-alpha colour and
`--selection-checked-border` is `var(--selection-border)`, so the control alone shows the selection, as
the customer-facing radio card does. Stacked rows keep a grey separator: both dividers are
`var(--border)`, checked or not. The border keeps its width, so rows join and inset as they do in
neutral themes. Without an outer edge, a row on a `card`-coloured surface, such as inside a white
`Card`, has no outline, and the checked row's `bg-muted` tint barely shows on the row's card fill. The
edge and the dividers are theme roles of their own, so changing them leaves `--border`, and the tables
and `CheckboxCard` it draws, as they are. To change the row edge or the dividers, override the
selection roles, or `--border`, on the element that carries the theme attributes, which is `<html>` or
a `ThemeScope`. The theme rules resolve the selection roles there. On a plain wrapper a `--border`
override reaches `CheckboxCard`, but the rows keep the edge and dividers the theme element resolved.

A disabled `RadioItem` or `CheckboxItem` row, whether the row or its group is disabled, fills with
`--selection-disabled-background` under a 45° hatch striped in `--selection-disabled-hatch`. Neutral
themes fill it with `var(--muted)` and stripe it in black at 2% alpha, which shows in light schemes.
Branded themes keep the card fill of an enabled row, `var(--card)`, and make the stripe a zero-alpha
colour, so a disabled row is not a grey panel among white rows; instead its title and other label row
text grey to `--selection-disabled-foreground`, `var(--muted-foreground)`. Neutral themes set that
role to `var(--foreground)`, so their titles keep their colour. A description keeps its own muted text,
a SubSection keeps its colours, and the control dims to 50% in every theme. The role is not text-grade,
so it has no contrast floor: WCAG exempts an inactive control. The disabled fill replaces a checked
row's `bg-muted` tint and a background class on the row; the checked edge stays. Neutral themes
resolve the fill's `var(--muted)` on the element that carries the theme attributes, so override
`--selection-disabled-background` itself to change the fill under a plain wrapper.

A pressed `Toggle` or `ToggleGroup.Item` fills with `--toggle-pressed` and sets its label in
`--toggle-pressed-foreground`. Neutral themes keep the quiet `var(--muted)` face with
`var(--foreground)` text on their neutral page. Branded themes fill it with `var(--primary)` and
`var(--primary-foreground)`, because `muted` barely shows on a tinted brand page and is also the hover
tint. Both pairs are aliases, so a dark scheme resolves them against its own roles. A pressed toggle
keeps its face under the pointer. To change it, override the two roles on the element that carries the
theme attributes, or on any element around the toggles.

The weight of a `RadioItem` or `CheckboxItem` row title is themed too. It, which reads
`--selection-title-weight`. Branded themes set it to `500`, the weight of the group legend and a
`CheckboxCard` title, and neutral themes keep the regular `400`. A `className` weight on the title
replaces the theme's.

A `Text`'s line height is themed at three sizes. Without a `leading` prop, `size="sm"`, the default
size and `size="lg"` read `--paragraph-leading-sm`, `--paragraph-leading-base` and
`--paragraph-leading-lg`. Branded themes set the paragraph styles 14/20, 16/22 and 18/24, and neutral
themes keep the relaxed 1.625 ratio, 22.75, 26 and 29.25px. The other sizes stay relaxed in every
theme. A `leading` prop or a `className` leading replaces the role, and `Span` keeps its own snug
default.

A `Heading`'s default type is themed at levels 1 to 3. Without a `size` prop, an h1 reads
`--heading-1-size` and `--heading-1-leading`, and without a `font` prop it reads `--heading-1-weight`,
and h2 and h3 read their own roles. Branded themes set h1 to the heading small style, 24/32, and h2 and
h3 to heading xsmall, 20/32, all at `700`. Neutral themes keep h1 at 24/33, h2 at 18/24.75 and h3 at
16/22, all at `500`. h4 to h6 stay 16/22 at `500` in every theme. A `size` prop replaces a level's
size and line height, a `font` prop its weight, and a `className` size or weight replaces the role.

Themes set no fonts. Fuse sets body text in `--font-sans` and headings in `--font-heading`, and your
app sets both, as [the fonts page](/handbook/fonts) shows.

The modals, `Dialog`, `AlertDialog` and `Sheet`, fill with `--dialog` and set their text in
`--dialog-foreground`. Neutral themes and every dark theme alias the popover pair, so a modal looks
like the anchored popups. Branded light themes fill a modal with the page tint, `var(--background)`,
in the page's `var(--foreground)`, so the white fields and selection rows inside it keep the separation
they have on the page. Popover, Select, Combobox, DropdownMenu, NavigationMenu, Toolbar, the neutral
Toast and the `Command` palette stay on `--popover`. The menus among them highlight rows with the
accent step, which needs the white popup. The theme rules resolve `--dialog` on the element that
carries the theme attributes, so a `--popover` override on a plain wrapper no longer reaches a modal:
override `--dialog` there instead. To put one modal back on the popover fill, pass
`className="bg-popover text-popover-foreground"`.

The sidebar's brand pair, `--sidebar-brand` and `--sidebar-brand-foreground`, reaches 4.5:1 in every
theme and color scheme. Where the brand color falls short on the sidebar, the theme steps its
lightness away from the sidebar's until it passes and keeps the hue, so the tone can differ between
light and dark. `--brand` keeps the brand color everywhere else.

The stylesheet build computes the stepped tones, so the stylesheet declares them as literal colors.
Fjordkraft, Fjordkraft Företag and TrøndelagKraft have one in light themes, and Gudbrandsdal Energi,
Telinet, Elmera and Nordic Green Energy have one in dark themes. In those themes a host override of
`--brand` no longer moves `--sidebar-brand`. In every dark theme, `--sidebar-brand-foreground` is the
sidebar color instead of `--brand-foreground`. To restyle the sidebar brand, override both sidebar
tokens on the element that carries the theme attributes, in a rule after the Fuse stylesheet. The rule
applies in both color schemes and both identities, so give the dark scheme its own pair in a second
rule. That rule needs two selectors, one for a theme element under a dark ancestor and one for an
element that is dark itself, such as a themed `<html>`.

{/* prettier-ignore */}
```css
/* After the Fuse stylesheet, on the element that carries the theme attributes. */
.app-shell[data-theme-brand="tkas"] {
  --sidebar-brand: oklch(0.45 0.08 191);
  --sidebar-brand-foreground: oklch(1 0 0);
}

/* No one tone reaches 4.5:1 on both sidebars, so dark takes its own pair.
   The first selector matches under a dark ancestor, the second an element
   that is dark itself, such as a themed <html>. */
[data-theme="dark"] .app-shell[data-theme-brand="tkas"],
.app-shell[data-theme="dark"][data-theme-brand="tkas"] {
  --sidebar-brand: oklch(0.75 0.08 191);
  --sidebar-brand-foreground: oklch(0.205 0 0);
}
```

Brand-colored text, the `brand` variant of `Heading`, `Text`, `Link` and `Span`, takes `--brand-ink`.
The build steps it the same way until it reaches 4.5:1 on both the page and a card, `--background` and
`--card`. Fjordkraft, Fjordkraft Företag and TrøndelagKraft have a stepped tone in light themes, and
Gudbrandsdal Energi, Telinet, Elmera and Nordic Green Energy have one in dark themes. In those themes a
host override of `--brand` no longer moves brand text. To restyle it, override `--brand-ink` with the
same selectors as the sidebar pair above. In every theme, the theme rules resolve `--brand-ink` on the
element that carries the theme attributes, so a `--brand` override on a plain wrapper inside it no
longer reaches brand text. Set `--brand-ink` on that wrapper as well.

<h2 id="inner-corners">Inner corners</h2>

A part laid against a padded surface's edge rounds concentrically with the surface: its corner is the
surface's corner less the padding and border between them, and never below 0. The part that pads it
publishes that corner as `--inner-corner`, and the inner part rounds with the `rounded-inner` utility.
Free-standing controls in a surface's content, such as a dialog's buttons, keep their own corner. The
publishing parts are:

- Menus: `DropdownMenu.Content` and `DropdownMenu.SubContent`; `Combobox.List`, not
  `Combobox.Content`; `Select.Group`, and `Select.Content` for rows outside a group; and
  `NavigationMenu.Content` in its popup.
- Fields: every field box, `InputGroup.Addon`, `Combobox.Chips` and the date fields' segment row.
- Containers: `Tabs.List`, `Toolbar.Root`, `Frame.Root` and `Frame.Panel`, the floating `Sidebar`'s
  header, footer and groups, and the date pickers' preset group.
- Surfaces: `Card` and its sections, `Dialog.Content`, `Popover.Content`, `Tooltip.Content`, toasts,
  `Item`, `SelectionItem`, `Empty`, the rounded `Accordion` items and the standalone `Calendar`.

Put a custom row or block inside one of these parts and give it `rounded-inner`, and it rounds like the
parts beside it. Anywhere else, such as directly in `Combobox.Content`, `rounded-inner` rounds with the
theme's `--radius`. A `ThemeScope` resets it, so a part inside a nested scope rounds with that scope's
radius.

<h2 id="document-theme">The document theme</h2>

A page sets its brand once, on `<html>`, and mounts one `ThemeProvider` with the same theme. The
provider is fully controlled: there is no `setTheme` for identity, brand or segment. A host changes
brand by passing a new `theme` prop.

{/* prettier-ignore */}
```tsx
import { documentAttributes, ThemeProvider } from "@elmeragroup/fuse/theme";

const THEME = { identity: "branded", brand: "fkas", segment: "private" } as const;

<html {...documentAttributes({ theme: THEME, density: "comfortable" })}>
  <body>
    <ThemeProvider theme={THEME}>{children}</ThemeProvider>
  </body>
</html>;
```

Density is stamped here too, and is not a theme axis. `documentAttributes` takes the theme and a
required density, because no identity implies one: the document resolves one density for the whole
page. See [Density](/handbook/density) for an app that needs a dense dashboard and comfortable promo
pages.

<h2 id="theme-scope">Scoped re-theming</h2>

`ThemeScope` re-themes a subtree. It is never a document writer: it does not stamp `<html>`, does not
fork color-scheme state, and has no density prop.

{/* prettier-ignore */}
```tsx
import { ThemeScope } from "@elmeragroup/fuse/theme";

// Re-themes this subtree only. Overlays opened inside it portal into it.
<ThemeScope theme={{ identity: "branded", brand: "tkas", segment: "company" }}>
  <TrackSummary />
</ThemeScope>;
```

The containment problem a scope creates is solved at the component layer. `ThemeScope` publishes its
rendered element through context, and every overlay resolves its portal target as: explicit
`container` → nearest scope element → the primitive default. A popup opened inside a scope therefore
lands inside it and inherits its theme, instead of escaping to `document.body` and silently taking the
page theme. The [Theme matrix](/handbook/theme-matrix) demonstrates this twenty-four times over.

`useTheme()` returns the nearest theme plus its slug, and throws outside a provider or scope.

<h2 id="color-scheme">Color scheme</h2>

Light and dark are separate from the three theme axes. Color-scheme state lives on the document
writer; scopes do not fork it. Emit `ColorSchemeScript` in `<head>` ahead of anything paintable so the
stored preference applies before first paint, and leave the provider's `injectColorSchemeScript` at its
default, `false`, so the bootstrap is emitted exactly once.

<h2 id="first-paint">First paint in your framework</h2>

Use the [Quick start scaffold](/quick-start#page-scaffold) for the shared setup. Pass the same resolved
theme to the document attributes and provider, and stamp density on the document root. Keep
`storageKey`, `defaultColorScheme`, `enableSystem` and any `forcedColorScheme` identical on the
bootstrap and provider. ThemeProvider supplies context and runtime updates; the host places the classic
bootstrap before paintable content.

{/* prettier-ignore */}
```css
html, body {
  background: var(--background);
  color: var(--foreground);
}
```

Import `themes.css` alongside your chosen CSS mode. The token-backed canvas prevents a default browser
background flash. Put `suppressHydrationWarning` on the React-owned html element when the bootstrap
changes data-theme before hydration. If you omit color-scheme support, omit the script, warning and
provider color-scheme options together.

Pass a nonce to the bootstrap for nonce-based CSP. Keep the generated bootstrap in a server or
build-time module rather than copying its IIFE into application source. The library uses local
storage, not cookies, and CSS owns native color-scheme. Hash-based CSP is unsupported. React-created
script nodes do not provide first paint.

A route that must first-paint in a forced scheme needs a document adapter that knows the force while
generating HTML and passes forcedColorScheme to both bootstrap and provider. A descendant
ForceColorScheme changes the document only at runtime.

Next App Router and Vite have production first-paint fixtures in apps/docs and apps/static-theme,
including delayed or blocked React execution. Next Pages, TanStack Start and React Router below are
written recipes without fixture verification. These first-paint proofs are separate from packed-package
release fixtures.

<h2 id="next-app-router">Next App Router</h2>

The Quick start layout puts ColorSchemeScript in head. This also avoids the hidden streaming preamble
Next can insert at the start of body. Leave the provider's `injectColorSchemeScript` at its default,
false. A forced route needs a route-group layout or other document that supplies the same force to
both.

<h2 id="next-pages">Next Pages</h2>

The document owns html attributes and the classic script; the app owns the provider. Share theme and
color-scheme options through an app-owned module. Put the script in Head or before Main. A route force
must be known to the document and the app provider.

{/* prettier-ignore */}
```tsx
// pages/_document.tsx
import { Head, Html, Main, NextScript } from "next/document";
import { ColorSchemeScript, documentAttributes } from "@elmeragroup/fuse/theme";
import { colorScheme, density, theme } from "../lib/theme";

export default function Document() {
  return (
    <Html lang="nb" {...documentAttributes({ theme, density })} suppressHydrationWarning>
      <Head>
        <ColorSchemeScript
          storageKey={colorScheme.storageKey}
          defaultColorScheme={colorScheme.defaultColorScheme}
          enableSystem={colorScheme.enableSystem}
        />
      </Head>
      <body>
        <Main />
        <NextScript />
      </body>
    </Html>
  );
}

// pages/_app.tsx
import type { AppProps } from "next/app";
import { ThemeProvider } from "@elmeragroup/fuse/theme";
import { colorScheme, theme } from "../lib/theme";

export default function App({ Component, pageProps }: AppProps) {
  return (
    <ThemeProvider
      theme={theme}
      storageKey={colorScheme.storageKey}
      defaultColorScheme={colorScheme.defaultColorScheme}
      enableSystem={colorScheme.enableSystem}
      injectColorSchemeScript={false}>
      <Component {...pageProps} />
    </ThemeProvider>
  );
}
```

<h2 id="tanstack-start">TanStack Start</h2>

The root document owns attributes. Call colorSchemeScriptSource at document-render time and place
ScriptOnce before children and module scripts. A route-specific force goes into both that call and
ThemeProvider.

{/* prettier-ignore */}
```tsx
import { ScriptOnce } from "@tanstack/react-router";
import { colorSchemeScriptSource, documentAttributes, ThemeProvider } from "@elmeragroup/fuse/theme";
import { colorScheme, density, theme } from "./theme";

export function RootDocument({ children }: { children: React.ReactNode }) {
  return (
    <html lang="nb" {...documentAttributes({ theme, density })} suppressHydrationWarning>
      <head>
        <ScriptOnce>{colorSchemeScriptSource(colorScheme)}</ScriptOnce>
      </head>
      <body>
        <ThemeProvider theme={theme} {...colorScheme} injectColorSchemeScript={false}>
          {children}
        </ThemeProvider>
      </body>
    </html>
  );
}
```

<h2 id="react-router">React Router 7 framework mode</h2>

The root Layout owns attributes and emits ColorSchemeScript in head before Meta or Links content that
depends on the marker. A route force must reach both the head script and provider while rendering the
document.

{/* prettier-ignore */}
```tsx
import { Links, Meta, Scripts, ScrollRestoration } from "react-router";
import { ColorSchemeScript, documentAttributes, ThemeProvider } from "@elmeragroup/fuse/theme";
import { colorScheme, density, theme } from "./theme";

export function Layout({ children }: { children: React.ReactNode }) {
  return (
    <html lang="nb" {...documentAttributes({ theme, density })} suppressHydrationWarning>
      <head>
        <ColorSchemeScript
          storageKey={colorScheme.storageKey}
          defaultColorScheme={colorScheme.defaultColorScheme}
          enableSystem={colorScheme.enableSystem}
        />
        <Meta />
        <Links />
      </head>
      <body>
        <ThemeProvider
          theme={theme}
          storageKey={colorScheme.storageKey}
          defaultColorScheme={colorScheme.defaultColorScheme}
          enableSystem={colorScheme.enableSystem}
          injectColorSchemeScript={false}>
          {children}
        </ThemeProvider>
        <ScrollRestoration />
        <Scripts />
      </body>
    </html>
  );
}
```

<h2 id="vite">Vite and client-rendered apps</h2>

Stamp brand and density into index.html at build time. Inject a raw classic script before the module
entry through transformIndexHtml with order set to post. The apps/static-theme fixture demonstrates
this adapter.

Vite's React template sets `--border` and `--accent` on `:root` in `src/index.css`, and both are names
of Fuse role tokens. When that stylesheet loads after `themes.css`, its `:root` rule beats a neutral
theme, so delete those variables or rename them.

1. Import documentAttributes and colorSchemeScriptSource in vite.config.ts, and pass the page's
   density explicitly. Use the same document options as the React provider. Keep these calls out of
   the client graph.
2. Stamp the three brand attributes and data-density on html. Reject source HTML that already has them
   so configuration cannot silently overwrite another owner.
3. Put the generated classic bootstrap before the first module script. Hoist the generated stylesheet
   links before it so background tokens exist before first paint. Keep the token-backed canvas rule in
   the global stylesheet.
4. Mount ThemeProvider with matching options, leaving injectColorSchemeScript at its default, false. A
   bundling config loader can rewrite Function.prototype.toString and break the closed IIFE. The
   fixture uses the native config loader with the built theme module.
5. For route-specific forced first paint, use a separate HTML entry or a transform that resolves the
   route and passes the same force to bootstrap and provider.

<h2 id="own-tokens">Keeping your own tokens</h2>

A host that adopts one component at a time can keep its own design tokens and skip `themes.css`. Every
component still needs `@elmeragroup/fuse/css` for its variants and control metrics. Import it before
your own `@theme` block, so your values win where both define a theme variable, then map the role
tokens the components you use read.

{/* prettier-ignore */}
```css
/* app/globals.css */
@import "tailwindcss";
@import "@elmeragroup/fuse/css";
@source "../node_modules/@elmeragroup/fuse";

@theme {
  /* Your tokens. After fuse/css, so yours win where both define a theme variable. */
}

:root {
  /* The role tokens the components you use read: whole colours, not channel triplets. */
  --primary: var(--your-brand);
  --primary-foreground: #fff;
  --radius: 0.5rem;
  --radius-button: var(--radius);
}
```

Each component page lists those tokens under "Tokens consumed"; map that list, and add the next
component's when you adopt it. Role tokens are whole colours in any notation, never channel triplets.
Three things in `fuse/css` reach your own utilities as well, and the package README lists them in
full: the `neutral-*` palette is remapped onto `--neutral-*`, which only `themes.css` defines; the
`rounded-*` rungs become `--radius-step` multiples from `--radius`, with a 0px step fallback so your
one radius applies everywhere; and the `data-open`, `data-selected`, `data-disabled` and related state
variants are redeclared at one-class specificity.

<h2 id="in-these-docs">In these docs</h2>

`data-theme="dark"` activates dark palettes on both the document and nested theme scopes. Neutral
themes use neutral colors; branded themes use their brand palettes. The
[theme matrix](/handbook/theme-matrix) has light, dark, and system controls for comparing them. Colour
scheme does not change theme slugs, density or brand primitives.

This site is a worked example of the split. Its own document theme is fixed at `neutral-elma-private`
and the chrome you are reading consumes that theme's light and dark tokens. The header's theme settings
menu writes docs-local preview state from its labelled radio groups (Appearance, Identity, Brand,
Segment). Identity, Brand and Segment feed demo stages and theme scopes only, so every demo can be
viewed in all twenty-four permutations while the page keeps its Elmera identity. Appearance changes the
whole document through the library's color-scheme API, offers light, dark and system, and remembers
your choice; the matrix mirrors the same three-way control.
