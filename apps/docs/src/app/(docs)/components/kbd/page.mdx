<Prose>

`Kbd` renders one key as a `<kbd>` element. `KbdGroup` wraps the caps of a combination in an outer `<kbd>`, which is how HTML marks keys pressed together. A cap holds whatever you give it: a word, a glyph such as `⌘`, or an icon.

Kbd renders the glyph you pass and never detects the platform, because the server cannot know the visitor's operating system and a guess would differ from the browser's render. When your app knows the platform, pass `⌘` or `Ctrl` yourself.

Screen readers read a cap. When a cap only repeats a shortcut, set `aria-hidden` on it and put the shortcut on the control it belongs to in `aria-keyshortcuts`, as the search field below does.

A cap has one size at both densities and takes its colours from the muted role tokens. In an `InputGroup` addon, a cap or a group is the addon's direct child and rounds with the addon's inner corner. A group passes that corner on to its caps.

</Prose>
