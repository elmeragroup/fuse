<Prose>

`Toolbar.Root` paints the bar: a bordered popover fill with 4px of padding between its edge and
its items. Name it with `aria-label`. Tab moves focus into the bar and out of it again. Inside,
the arrow keys move between items and wrap at the ends. Set `loopFocus={false}` to stop at the
ends instead.

`Toolbar.Button`, `Toolbar.Link` and `Toolbar.Input` only place an item in that focus order.
Each takes its look from the control it renders, such as
`render={<Button variant="ghost" size="sm" />}`. A disabled item stays in the arrow-key order,
so a keyboard user still finds it. The bar publishes `--inner-corner`, so a custom block in it
that rounds with `rounded-inner` sits concentric with the bar.

For a toolbar that only switches between options, use ToggleGroup on its own.

</Prose>

`Toolbar.Group` lays out related items together and disables them all with `disabled`.
`Toolbar.Separator` draws a line across the bar between groups.

<Demo slug="toolbar" id="toggle-group" title="Toggle group" file="toolbar-toggle-group.tsx">
  <ToolbarToggleGroup />
</Demo>

A ToggleGroup inside a toolbar joins the toolbar's arrow-key order instead of keeping its own.
A single Toggle joins it through `Toolbar.Button`'s `render`.

<Demo slug="toolbar" id="popups" title="Menus, popovers and tooltips" file="toolbar-popups.tsx">
  <ToolbarPopups />
</Demo>

A Popover or DropdownMenu trigger goes in `Toolbar.Button`'s `render`. A tooltip wraps the
other way round: pass the `Toolbar.Button` to `Tooltip.Trigger`'s `render`.

<Demo slug="toolbar" id="input" title="Links and an input" file="toolbar-input.tsx">
  <ToolbarInput />
</Demo>

The arrow keys move a text input's caret as well as the toolbar's focus. Put at most one
`Toolbar.Input` in a horizontal toolbar, as its last item.

<Demo slug="toolbar" id="vertical" title="Vertical" file="toolbar-vertical.tsx">
  <ToolbarVertical />
</Demo>

With `orientation="vertical"` the bar stacks its items, the up and down arrows move between
them, and separators turn horizontal.
