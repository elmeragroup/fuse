## Skeleton from the content

<Prose>

A hand-placed `Skeleton` repeats the layout of what it stands in for. `SkeletonReveal` draws the
skeleton from the content itself: while loading, it renders its children with `sample` data,
unseen, and draws a bar over each line of their text and a block over each control, avatar, badge
or image. The skeleton takes exactly the content's space, so the page does not move when the data
arrives.

Pass `data` and a render function. The skeleton shows while `data` is `null` or `undefined`, drawn
from the last data shown, or from `sample` before any has loaded. Set `loading` to show it over
data that is refetching; it is then drawn from the data on screen. Plain children work too, with
`loading`.

The root is busy while loading and reads `label` to screen readers, the dictionary's "Loading" by
default. The skeleton is hidden from them and inert, and the content can take focus once it
replaces the skeleton. Form controls in the skeleton are not submitted or validated with their
form, and Fuse overlays in it, such as a Dialog left open, do not open. Keep any portal of your own
in the content closed while it loads. Before the page hydrates, the skeleton strikes a thick bar through each line
of text. Mark your own markup with `data-skeleton="block"` to draw it as one block, or
`data-skeleton="ignore"` to leave it out.

</Prose>

<Prose>

`reveal` decides how the content replaces the skeleton. `smart`, the default, holds a skeleton back
for 150 ms so a fast load never flashes one. It brings content in at once while someone scrolls or
types, crossfades a refetch and small content, and brings larger content in row by row. `together`
crossfades everything at once, and `sequence` brings each piece in from top to bottom.

</Prose>

reduced motion the height changes at once.
