Wrap each term and its details in an `Item`, the `<div>` HTML allows around one name-value group. Every list is made of Items: HTML doesn't allow a list to mix Items with bare terms and details, the inline layout styles terms and details only inside an Item, and both layouts draw rules between Items only. An Item takes attributes such as a `data-testid`, so a test can find a row by it.

An Item can hold several terms of one details, or several details of one term. Its last term shares a row with its first details; earlier terms and later details take rows of their own. Rules fall between Items only.

`layout="inline"` is for label/value rows in receipts, order summaries and price boxes. Inline rows take the label type, so they follow density like field labels, are spaced by the small surface gap and have no rules. The term is not muted; add `text-muted-foreground` to a term to mute it, or `font-medium` to a details to emphasise it. To group rows, use one inline list per group: consecutive inline lists are spaced by the large surface gap. A colour class on a term or details wins in both layouts. The inline cells' padding, sizing and alignment come from the list, so override them with a `[:where([data-layout=inline]>*)>&]:` class or a CSS rule. Override an inline Item's row, wrap and gap with a `[:where([data-layout=inline])>&]:` class.

Each inline Item shares its own row between its term and details, whatever the other rows hold. The details sits beside its term, at the end of the row. When the two don't fit on one line, both wrap within the row: each keeps its longest word and they share the rest, so a long term wraps beside a short details, and a long details wraps beside a short term. Only when the term's longest word and the details' longest word can't share the row does the details move under its term, still at the end of the row, rather than break a word. A term wraps at its spaces and hyphens, so a hyphenated label such as "Målepunkt-ID" can split at the hyphen beside a long value; write it with a non-breaking hyphen (U+2011) to keep it whole.

Values in a real order summary are often longer than their labels: a time with seconds, an 18-digit ID, an invoice line that ends in an email address. A value wraps between its words beside its term. An email address too wide to sit beside its term moves under it whole, and one wider than the whole row breaks inside it there rather than overflow the card; a `<wbr>` before the `@` chooses where.
