Wrap each term and its details in an `Item`, the `<div>` HTML allows around one name-value group. Every list is made of Items: HTML doesn't allow a list to mix Items with bare terms and details, and the layouts style terms and details only inside an Item. An Item takes attributes such as a `data-testid`, so a test can find a row by it.

</Demo>

An Item can hold several terms of one details, or several details of one term. Its last term shares a row with its first details; earlier terms and later details take rows of their own. Rules fall between Items only.

`layout="inline"` is for label/value rows in receipts, order summaries and price boxes. Inline rows take the label type, so they follow density like field labels, are spaced by the small surface gap and have no rules. The term is not muted; add `text-muted-foreground` to a term to mute it, or `font-medium` to a details to emphasise it. To group rows, use one inline list per group: consecutive inline lists are spaced by the large surface gap. A colour class on a term or details wins in both layouts. The inline cells' padding, sizing and alignment come from the list, so override them with a `[:where([data-layout=inline]>*)>&]:` class or a CSS rule, and an inline Item's row, wrap and gap with a `[:where([data-layout=inline])>&]:` class.

Each inline Item shares its own row between its term and details, whatever the other rows hold. The details sits at the end of the term's line while the two fit, and a long term wraps beside a short details. A details too wide to sit beside the term's longest word moves under the term, at the end of the row, so an email address or a date stays on one line. Only a details wider than the whole row wraps, between words where it can.
